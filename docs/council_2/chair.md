# Chair's own memo (written before reading any seat's memo)

**Position.** I do not know whether the edge is alive, and neither does the data we have: the record since December 2024 is indistinguishable from a coin flip (6.5% and 9.7% Combine passes against 16%, roughly 23 independent Combines, p about 0.1-0.3), and the strongest earlier evidence (2023-24) is a single good stretch in a hold-out that has been looked at many times. My guess, a judgement and not a computation, is that the chance the sleeves still carry enough edge to make the farm positive after fees is about one in four (priors: noise-area momentum replications are weaker than the original paper, dev t 1.6 to 2.2 for noise-area, gap-VWAP t 3.4 picked from 156 variants, 2025+ weak). That makes the next step an information problem, not another optimisation problem: nothing we have not already tried on dev can raise the odds by more than a few points, and every further dev look makes the selection bias worse.

**What I would do, ranked.**
1. **Start collecting clean evidence now, at zero cost.** Run the tick harness on an always-on machine against the live LSE stream (paper only). Make the forward test report what we could not measure before: sleeve R in R units (PR18) and Combine passes against the coin-flip benchmark (about 14-16%). Honest expectation: a decisive answer takes 250+ sessions; a STOP is a 2.8:1 signal at best, so this is a monitor, not a verdict. Before it starts: stress-test the tick path (reconnects, holes, DST, restarts), because the stream has never been seen.
2. **Pre-register and test the cheap Combine-stage ideas on dev only**, each with a hard stop: (a) consistency-aware management (the 55% rule blocked every 2025 H1 Combine that reached the target, so the ceiling is a few points of pass rate); (b) several sleeves traded together in one account instead of one sleeve per Combine (the user's observation; correlations of 0.48 to 0.74 suggest a small gain); (c) a lower Combine R on the 150K (the user's choice instead of a smaller account), noting the fee is billed per 21-session cycle, so slower Combines cost more. Expected total effect: a few percentage points of pass rate, not a different business.
3. **A paper-only pause/resume policy for Combine purchases** (the farm's real exposure is the $200-290 per try), so that a drought costs a budget, not a year of fees. Earlier work says such rules limit damage and create no profit; that is the point.
4. **Park new strategy research** (D1 re-scoring, D3 trend-day capture) unless the forward data or a high-prior idea justifies it. About 5,000 configurations are already in the selection ledger.

**What I would not do.** More dev grids; adopting the VWAP sleeve because it won the last two years; regime switching (nine causal variants beat nothing); aggressive sizing (it lowers pass odds); any real-money step before the forward gates in the pre-registration are met.

**Biggest thing I may be wrong about.** That waiting is cheap. A year of paper trading is a long time for the user and a STOP has only 23% to 66% power by then; if the paper farm shows a pass rate near 15% after 20 Combines we still will not know whether the edge is gone or just small. I may also be wrong about my own diagnostics from this session: the coin-flip control trades every day at the open (the real sleeves trade 30-60% of days with their own entries), the 'blocked by consistency' count is a mechanical replay, and the post-December-2024 comparison is one overlapping year.

**Questions for the user.** Can an always-on machine of yours run the harness (a computer left on, a spare device)? Is a year-long forward test acceptable as the decision path? Is there any trigger (a number of failed Combines, a date) after which you would want the project parked rather than extended?
