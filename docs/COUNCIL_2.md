## 1. Where the seats agree
1. **Stop the analysis phase.** No seat found a Combine-mechanics lever (mechanics), a strategy or basket gain (strategy), a regime signal (earlier work, advocate's check of the switcher code) or a further test of the data that would move belief by more than about 2:1 (statistician, advocate). Chance that a further intraday idea adds a real 0.2 Sharpe: 3 to 5% (strategy). More dev looks only add multiplicity (about 5,000 already).
2. **The data cannot say whether the edge is alive.** 2025-26 shows no break from 2011-2024: shortfall 1.1 to 1.5 standard errors, p 0.10 to 0.30 allowing every break date, Bayes factor for a break 0.4 to 1.2 (statistician); one bad year is a likelihood ratio of at most 2.5 (advocate). Statistician's probabilities for today's net edge: dead 20%, at least break-even 70%, at least half of dev 50%; the advocate puts weights giving a farm mean of +$2k to +$4k and a median of -$2k to -$3k over 3 years before Topstep discretion; the chair's judgement was 25% for 'farm positive after fees'. These are not in conflict: they define the event differently. Nothing available settles it in under about 3.5 years.
3. **Start the free clock.** A paper forward test on live ticks is the only new information and costs nothing but an always-on machine. It is a monitor with a kill line: it can stop a failing strategy, 'not stopped' at 250 sessions is only a 2.3:1 signal, and confirming a full edge takes about 3.9 years (ops, statistician).
4. **No real money now.** Decide real money by a loss budget the user sets, never by significance or a first payout (statistician, farm, advocate). A first Combine passes 13 to 27%: a lottery ticket, not a test.
5. **Keep R = 9,600.** A lower R on the 150K (the user's choice instead of a smaller account) raises the pass rate by 5 points (noise-area) and 8 (gap-VWAP) at R = 4,800, but Combines last longer (1.3 to 1.7 and 1.5 to 2.4 fee cycles), cost per pass stays at $1,000 to $1,300 and EV per session falls 30 to 40% (mechanics). The consistency rule is not fixable by sizing: a daily cap just under $4,950 lowers pass by 2 to 4 points; shrinking when blocked changes nothing.

## 2. Corrections the council forced (also FINDINGS 37)
* **The chair's coin-flip benchmark was wrong [chair re-run].** The coin flip's daily R volatility is 0.61 against 0.31 and 0.19; a risk-matched coin passes about 17% (dev), 20-21% (2023-24 starts), 20-22% (since 2024-12), and a shape-matched null about 2-3% (advocate). Pass rate cannot test the edge; the 14-16% benchmark and the claim 'no better than a coin flip' are withdrawn. The mechanics seat's reading of the 2025 pass rates as 'edge decay' relies on the same benchmark and is not supported.
* '6 of 42 blocked by the consistency rule' is about 3 events (advocate); the post-December-2024 farms are about one independent year with a cut date chosen after the outcomes (statistician, advocate); the hold-out 'ranking flip' is inside one standard error; the exact re-basing headline moves from $17.7k to $45.3k mean with the reference price (advocate).
* **Chair's data claim corrected [chair re-run].** The three raw Databento files are byte-identical to the assets of the repository's GitHub release `v1.0.0` (sha256 compared), so the raw data is not only in the container, and the 'back up Databento first' step of the ops memo is mostly moot: the derived session caches and the repaired copy should be rebuildable from the raw text with `market_data.py` and `repair_splices.py` (not re-tested here). The same fact raises a question for the user: the release is attached to a public repository and shows 5 to 6 downloads (see section 5).
* **The ops seat's stress test found six silent defects in the live path, and one is the chair's own earlier bug [chair re-run]:** 34 perfect Databento sessions closed at 13:15 ET (day after Thanksgiving, 24 December, 3 July) and the early-close rule accepted only 13:00 closes, so a flawless feed reads INVALID on those days, including at the first PR18 look.
